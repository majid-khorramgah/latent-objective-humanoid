# 01 — Research Question

## Learning Latent Human Objectives for Generalizable Humanoid Intelligence

**Date:** 14 August 2026

---

## 1. Research Question

The central research question is:

> **Can an objective inferred from human locomotion demonstrations remain meaningful when it is re-optimized under the dynamics and physical constraints of a different humanoid robot?**

The target robot for the initial investigation is the Unitree H1, simulated in NVIDIA Isaac Lab.

The intended framework is:

    Human Locomotion Demonstrations
                ↓
        Objective Inference
                ↓
       Human-Derived Objective
                ↓
        H1 Dynamics + Constraints
                ↓
              MPC
                ↓
          H1 Locomotion
                ↓
           Generalization

---

## 2. Why This Question?

The original motivation is to move beyond direct trajectory imitation.

A human demonstration provides a trajectory, but the trajectory is generated by a human body with human-specific:

- morphology,
- dynamics,
- actuation,
- contacts,
- and physical constraints.

Therefore, directly reproducing the human trajectory on a humanoid robot may require the robot to imitate behavior that is physically specific to the human body.

Instead, this project asks whether the information that is more transferable is the underlying objective of the behavior.

Conceptually:

    Human Trajectory
          ↓
    What was the human trying to optimize?
          ↓
    Human Objective
          ↓
    Optimize under H1 physics
          ↓
    H1-generated motion

The important idea is therefore:

> **Transfer the objective rather than the trajectory.**

---

## 3. Why This Question Follows the Literature Review

The literature review identified two established research directions.

### 3.1 Objective Inference from Demonstrations

IOC and IRL demonstrate that behavior can be used to infer an underlying objective, cost, or reward.

Conceptually:

    Observed Behavior
          ↓
    Objective / Reward

However, existing methods often depend on assumptions about:

- candidate features,
- cost structures,
- reward representations,
- or the system being modeled.

This leaves an important question for humanoid locomotion:

> Can a human-derived objective be useful when the system executing it is not the human?

---

### 3.2 Model-Based Humanoid Control

Model-based control and MPC can optimize motion while explicitly respecting the robot's:

- dynamics,
- contacts,
- actuation limits,
- kinematic constraints,
- and physical feasibility.

Conceptually:

    Objective
        +
    Robot Dynamics
        +
    Constraints
        ↓
       MPC
        ↓
    Robot Motion

Therefore, the literature provides the two main components separately:

    Human Demonstrations
          ↓
    Objective Inference


    Objective
        +
    Robot Dynamics
        +
    Constraints
        ↓
       MPC

The research question investigates whether these components can be meaningfully connected.

---

## 4. Why Not Direct Trajectory Imitation?

The project is deliberately different from:

    Human Trajectory
          ↓
    Trajectory Imitation
          ↓
    H1 Motion

This approach attempts to reproduce the observed human motion.

Our proposed direction is:

    Human Trajectory
          ↓
    Infer Objective
          ↓
    H1 Dynamics + Constraints
          ↓
         MPC
          ↓
    H1 Motion

The H1 is therefore allowed to generate a different trajectory if that trajectory satisfies the inferred objective under its own physical constraints.

This distinction is central to the project.

---

## 5. Why Human Locomotion?

The initial focus is locomotion because it provides a meaningful test of objective transfer.

Walking is not simply a matter of reproducing joint trajectories.

It involves interactions between:

- whole-body motion,
- balance,
- contact with the ground,
- energy expenditure,
- stability,
- task requirements,
- and physical constraints.

The literature also indicates that human locomotion should not automatically be described by one predefined objective.

Therefore, the project will not assume beforehand that human locomotion optimizes:

    Energy
    + Stability
    + Smoothness
    + Robustness

These are only candidate hypotheses.

The actual objective representation remains an open research question.

---

## 6. Why Unitree H1?

The Unitree H1 provides a practical humanoid platform for testing the central hypothesis.

The project already has:

- an Isaac Lab simulation environment,
- a validated H1 model,
- and a trained H1 locomotion baseline.

This allows the research question to be investigated without requiring immediate access to a physical robot.

The important experimental difference is:

    Human
      ↓
    Human-derived objective

versus

    H1
      ↓
    Same objective
      ↓
    H1-specific dynamics and constraints

This creates a controlled setting for studying objective transfer.

---

## 7. Why Model-Based MPC?

MPC is used because the research question is specifically about whether a human-derived objective can be executed by a robot under different physical dynamics and constraints.

MPC provides a mechanism for explicitly optimizing:

    Learned Objective
          +
    H1 Dynamics
          +
    H1 Constraints

at execution time.

This is different from directly asking a neural policy to reproduce human motion.

MPC is therefore the **control mechanism for testing the objective-transfer hypothesis**, not the novelty by itself.

---

## 8. What Does "Generalization" Mean?

In this project, generalization does not initially mean generalizing to every humanoid robot or every possible task.

The first experimentally testable definition is narrower:

> **Can an objective inferred from a subset of human locomotion demonstrations produce useful H1 behavior under conditions that were not used during objective inference?**

Potential held-out conditions may include:

- different walking speeds,
- different task conditions,
- different locomotion contexts,
- or other controlled changes in the environment.

The exact generalization protocol will be defined later in this phase.

---

## 9. What We Are NOT Assuming

At this stage, we do not assume:

- that human locomotion has one universal objective;
- that the objective is energy minimization;
- that stability is necessarily an explicit objective;
- that smoothness is necessary;
- that robustness is part of the objective;
- that the objective must be a weighted sum of hand-designed features;
- that IOC is necessarily the final inference method;
- that IRL is necessarily the final inference method;
- that a neural latent representation is necessary;
- or that the final objective will transfer successfully to H1.

These are research questions to be tested rather than assumptions.

---

## 10. What Would Count as Evidence?

The core hypothesis would receive support if:

1. An objective can be inferred from human locomotion demonstrations.
2. The inferred objective explains held-out human behavior better than appropriate baselines.
3. The same objective can be optimized using H1 dynamics and constraints.
4. H1 can generate feasible locomotion without directly reproducing the human trajectory.
5. The resulting behavior remains useful under held-out conditions.
6. The objective-transfer approach provides an advantage over relevant baselines.

Importantly, simply generating a walking H1 is not sufficient evidence.

The experiment must test whether the **human-derived objective itself transfers**.

---

## 11. What Would Falsify or Weaken the Hypothesis?

The hypothesis would be weakened if:

- the inferred objective only explains the training demonstrations;
- the objective fails on held-out human motion;
- the objective cannot produce feasible H1 behavior;
- H1 requires extensive robot-specific modification of the objective;
- direct trajectory imitation performs equally well under the same evaluation;
- or the inferred objective does not generalize to changed conditions.

These failure cases are scientifically useful because they would indicate that the assumed notion of a transferable human objective is insufficient.

---

The literature review produced the following preliminary gap:

The present research question operationalizes that gap around a concrete experimental system:

    Human locomotion demonstrations

## 13. Current Research Question Status

**Status:** Preliminary but sufficiently specific to begin formal problem formulation.

The question is intentionally not locked to a particular learning algorithm or objective representation.

Those choices will be determined in the following steps based on:

- the literature,
- identifiability,
- available human data,
- computational feasibility,
- and experimental testability.
