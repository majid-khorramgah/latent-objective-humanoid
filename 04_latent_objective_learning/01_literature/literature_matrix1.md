# Literature Matrix — Phase 4.1

## Latent Human Objective Learning for Generalizable Humanoid Intelligence

This matrix summarizes the key literature relevant to the current research question:

> Can humanoid robots learn generalizable behaviors by discovering latent physical objectives underlying human motion rather than directly imitating trajectories?

The literature is organized around five areas:

1. Inverse Optimal Control (IOC)
2. Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)
3. Human Motion / Locomotion Objectives
4. Model-Based Control / MPC
5. RoMeLa / Dennis Hong / Humanoid Locomotion

---

# 1. Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)

| Paper | Main Problem | Input | What Is Learned? | Human Demonstrations? | Robot Control? | Generalization | Main Limitation for Our Project | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ng & Russell (2000) — *Algorithms for Inverse Reinforcement Learning* | Recover a reward function that explains observed behavior | Demonstrated behavior / expert policy | Reward function consistent with behavior | Possible, but not specifically human motion | No direct humanoid control | Limited | Classical formulation; reward ambiguity; does not address human locomotion or humanoid transfer | High |
| Ziebart et al. (2008) — *Maximum Entropy IRL* | Infer a reward while accounting for multiple plausible behaviors | Expert demonstrations | Reward distribution / reward function | Yes in principle | No | Better handling of behavioral ambiguity, but not cross-embodiment | Reward is still inferred within a specified feature/state representation | High |
| Wulfmeier et al. (2015/16) — *Maximum Entropy Deep IRL* | Learn more expressive reward functions using deep networks | Demonstrations + high-dimensional observations | Deep reward function | Yes | No direct humanoid control | More expressive than hand-designed linear rewards | Learned representation remains tied to training domain; no human-to-humanoid dynamics transfer | High |

### Main conclusion from IRL literature

IRL establishes that:

    Demonstrations
          ↓
    Infer Reward / Objective
          ↓
    Behavior Explanation

This is important for our project.

However, classical IRL does not by itself solve:

    Human Demonstrations
          ↓
    Generalizable Human Objective
          ↓
    Different Humanoid Dynamics
          ↓
    MPC

The major unresolved issue for our project is therefore not simply "can a reward be inferred?"

It is:

> Can an objective inferred from human locomotion remain meaningful when optimized under the dynamics and constraints of a different humanoid robot?

### Main conclusion from human locomotion literature

The locomotion literature gives us an important warning:

> We should not assume that human walking is generated by one simple objective.

Evidence suggests that human locomotion may involve multiple competing or simultaneous criteria.

Therefore, at the current stage we should NOT assume:

    Objective =
    Energy
    +
    Stability
    +
    Smoothness
    +
    Robustness

as a known fact.

These remain:

**Candidate hypotheses**

until the literature and experiments establish the appropriate representation.

    Objective
       +
    Robot Dynamics
       +
    Robot Constraints
       ↓

Therefore, model-based humanoid control itself is not the main novelty.

The potentially interesting research question lies earlier:

    Human Demonstrations

    MPC

# 6. What Has Already Been Established?

The current literature establishes several important facts.

### 6.1 Objective inference is possible

IOC/IRL literature establishes:

    Demonstrations
          ↓
    Objective / Reward

Therefore, the basic idea of inferring an objective from behavior is not novel by itself.

---

### 6.2 Human locomotion can be analyzed through objective functions

Human locomotion studies establish that:

    Human Walking
          ↓
    Candidate Cost Functions
          ↓
    Compare Explanatory Power

and that multiple movement goals may be relevant.

Therefore, simply proposing a cost function for human walking is not sufficient novelty.

---

### 6.3 Humanoid MPC is already established

Recent MPC literature establishes:

    Objective
       +
    Humanoid Dynamics
       +
    Constraints
       ↓
      MPC
       ↓
    Humanoid Motion

Therefore, using MPC for H1 is not by itself novel.

---

### 6.4 Humanoid RL is already established

Recent RoMeLa work establishes:

    Robot Model
       +
    Reward
       +
    RL
       ↓
    Humanoid Locomotion

Therefore, using RL for humanoid locomotion is also not novel.

---

# 7. What Has NOT Yet Been Established?

The literature reviewed so far does not establish the complete pipeline:

    Human Demonstrations
            ↓
    Generalizable Human Objective
            ↓
    Different Robot Dynamics
            +
    Robot Constraints
            ↓
    Model-Based MPC
            ↓
    Generalizable Humanoid Behavior

In particular, the following combination remains **not established yet** in our reviewed literature:

1. Human locomotion demonstrations.
2. Inference of an objective representing the underlying behavior.
3. Objective representation that is not completely tied to one specific robot.
4. Optimization of that objective under a different humanoid's dynamics.
5. Explicit handling of the target robot's physical constraints.
6. Generalization to unseen conditions or embodiments.

This is the area that requires the most careful investigation in the next literature stage.

---

# 8. Important Distinction: Reward Learning vs Objective Transfer

A central distinction emerging from the literature is:

### Robot-specific RL

    Robot A
      ↓
    Hand-designed Reward
      ↓
    RL
      ↓
    Policy A

This can work extremely well.

But the learned policy is generally tied to the robot and training environment.

---

### Our proposed direction

    Human Demonstrations
          ↓
    Objective Learning
          ↓
    Objective
          ↓
    Robot A Dynamics
          ↓
        MPC
          ↓
    Motion A

and potentially:

    Same Objective
          ↓
    Robot B Dynamics
          ↓
        MPC
          ↓
    Motion B

The research question is whether the objective can remain meaningful while the dynamics change.

This is the key conceptual distinction between:

**learning a policy**

and

**learning an objective that can be re-optimized for a new robot.**

# 9. Current Research Gap Status

## Established

The following are already established by prior work:

- IOC can infer objectives from demonstrations.
- IRL can infer rewards from demonstrations.
- Deep IRL can learn expressive reward functions.
- Human locomotion can be analyzed using cost functions.
- Human movement may involve multiple goals.
- Whole-body MPC can control legged/humanoid robots.
- Humanoid MPC can explicitly handle dynamics and constraints.
- RL can successfully learn humanoid locomotion.
- RoMeLa has demonstrated both model-based and learning-based humanoid locomotion.

---

## Not Established Yet

The following are **not yet established by the literature reviewed here**:

> A human locomotion objective learned from demonstrations can be transferred to a different humanoid embodiment and re-optimized through model-based MPC to produce generalizable behavior.

More specifically:

    Human Motion
        ↓
    Learned Objective
        ↓
    New Robot Dynamics
        ↓
    MPC
        ↓
    Generalizable Behavior

remains **Not Established Yet**.

This is a preliminary research gap, not yet a confirmed novelty claim.

---

# 10. Implications for Our Project

The literature suggests that our project should NOT be framed as:

> "We use IOC for humanoid locomotion."

This is too narrow and insufficiently novel.

It should also NOT be framed as:

> "We use MPC for humanoid locomotion."

This is already established.

It should NOT be framed as:

> "We use RL to learn humanoid locomotion."

This is also established.

The potentially stronger formulation is:

> **Can a human locomotion objective inferred from demonstrations be represented at a level that remains meaningful when re-optimized under the dynamics and physical constraints of a different humanoid robot?**

# 12. What We Should NOT Assume Yet

The literature does NOT currently justify assuming that the human objective is:

    Energy
      +
    Stability
      +
    Smoothness
      +
    Robustness
      +
    Task Success

These are only candidate components.

The correct objective representation is:

**Unknown**

until supported by the remaining literature and experiments.

Similarly, the following are still unknown:

- whether a single objective is sufficient;
- whether multiple objectives are necessary;
- whether objectives should be linear combinations;
- whether nonlinear representations are required;
- whether a latent variable is useful;
- whether the learned objective transfers across morphologies;
- whether the objective should be task-specific or general;
- whether MPC can optimize the learned representation efficiently enough for H1.

# 14. What the Literature Review Has Accomplished

The current literature has already ruled out several weak research directions.

### Weak direction 1

> Learn a reward from demonstrations.

Already established by IOC/IRL.

### Weak direction 2

> Use multiple cost functions to explain human movement.

Already established by human movement literature.

### Weak direction 3

> Use MPC for humanoid locomotion.

Already established.

### Weak direction 4

> Use RL for humanoid locomotion.

Already established.

### Weak direction 5

> Use robot-specific dynamics for humanoid control.

Already established.

Therefore, the project should focus on the **combination and transfer problem**, rather than any one of these components independently.

---

# 15. Current Preliminary Research Gap

Based only on the literature reviewed so far:

> **Preliminary Gap:** Existing work separately addresses objective/reward inference from demonstrations, analysis of human locomotion objectives, and model-based control or RL of humanoid robots. What remains insufficiently established is whether a human locomotion objective inferred from demonstrations can serve as a transferable behavioral representation that can be re-optimized under the dynamics and physical constraints of a different humanoid robot.

This is a **preliminary gap**.

It must NOT yet be presented as a definitive novelty claim.

---

# 16. Required Next Step

Before moving to Phase 4.2 — Problem Formulation, the literature review should verify this preliminary gap against the remaining relevant work.

The next question is:

> Has previous work already combined human objective inference with model-based control and demonstrated transfer to a different humanoid embodiment?

If the answer is **No**, this becomes a much stronger candidate research gap.

If the answer is **Yes**, we must identify exactly what limitation remains.
