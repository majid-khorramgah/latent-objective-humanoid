# Stage 34 - Latent Objective Discovery

## Overview

Previous stages focused on learning and evaluating human motion representations.

Completed stages:

- Human motion foundation dataset preparation
- Multi-branch motion foundation encoder
- Temporal and physics-aware latent representation learning
- Latent representation evaluation using UMAP and Linear Probe


The next challenge is moving from:

"How humans move"

towards:

"Why humans move"


This stage focuses on discovering the hidden objectives behind human motion.


---

# Research Motivation

Human motion is not only a sequence of joint movements.

Every movement is generated by underlying objectives such as:

- Energy efficiency
- Stability
- Safety
- Comfort
- Task completion


The goal of this stage is to investigate whether these hidden objectives can be discovered from learned motion representations.


---

# Core Research Question

How can latent objectives be discovered from human motion representations while ensuring that they are:

- Physically valid
- Human-like
- Task-oriented
- Generalizable


---

# Main Challenge

A major challenge is that mathematical optimization does not always produce human-like solutions.


For example:


Objective:

"Minimize energy consumption"


A robot may discover an unexpected solution:

- Crawling
- Crouching
- Unnatural locomotion


Although this solution minimizes energy, it does not represent natural human behavior.


Therefore, the main challenge is:


How can we discover objectives that optimize performance without allowing unrealistic shortcuts?


---

# Research Direction


The objective discovery process aims to learn:


Human Motion

↓

Motion Latent Representation

↓

Latent Objective Factors

↓

Objective-Aware Humanoid Behavior


---

# Expected Output


## 1. Objective Latent Representation

A latent space containing interpretable factors related to movement objectives.


Example:


Walking Motion:

Energy Efficiency: 0.8

Stability: 0.9

Speed: 0.5

Comfort: 0.7


---

## 2. Objective Factor Discovery

Identifying meaningful latent dimensions related to:


- Stability
- Energy efficiency
- Naturalness
- Task performance


---

## 3. Objective Validation

Testing whether modifying objective-related latent variables produces meaningful motion changes.


Example:


Increasing stability objective:


Expected:

- More balanced posture
- Reduced instability


Not expected:

- Unnatural movement strategies


---

# Open Research Question


Can we learn latent objectives that represent human movement preferences rather than only optimizing mathematical objectives?


The central challenge is discovering objectives that are:


Human-compatible

+

Physics-consistent

+

Task-oriented


This stage represents the transition from motion imitation toward objective-aware humanoid intelligence.
